Waves

Survive for as long as you can

Waves is the survival mode of Mad Orbs: you alone against wave after wave of bots, with no end. It runs in your own browser, with no server and no other players, on the map CTF-Invaders. On the start screen, click the red Waves card. The goal is simple: get as far as you can. Your record is the highest wave you reached, and it goes to the ranking.

And no run is wasted: each one earns Cores, which you trade in the Workshop for upgrades that stay for good. Every run starts a little stronger than the one before, like in a roguelite.

First time? The Waves tutorial (in the start screen's Tutorial shortcut, and offered the first time you open the mode) shows in about two minutes, with nothing able to hurt you, the waves, the crates and their upgrades, the grenades and the molotov, the weapons that come out of the crates and the Bomb.

The rules

  • Three lives (up to five with the Workshop). When you go down you are back by yourself 5 seconds later, while you have lives left. The clock of the wave stops while you are dead.
  • You start with the sub machine gun (or the weapon a milestone gave you), the knives, 2 grenades and a molotov. Better weapons come from the crates.
  • Each wave has a time limit: 30 seconds plus one per wave, up to a minute. When it runs out the next wave comes even if enemies are still alive, and they stay on the map.
  • A short break between waves: 9 seconds (5 before the first one).
  • The bots hunt you. They always know where you are and come for you, a little slower than you (70% of your speed). They don't break crates on purpose and don't take the power-ups. They come in at the map's edges: most on the side opposite you, a few elsewhere. About 4 in 10 charge right at you, and the brutes and the fast ones always do; the others, the sharpshooters and those with a sniper rifle fight from a distance.
  • Your life comes from the crates: hardly any fallen bot drops a life pack (7%; the boss always does), and it gives back a quarter of your life. Below half your life, the crates give life much more often. The bots drop no grenades either: only the crates give them.
  • They hit softer than in the other modes, a quarter of the damage, but every wave they hit harder and take longer to go down: on wave 10 they take as much as 1.6 times their life and hit 1.4 times as hard, on wave 20 2.3 and 1.8 times, on wave 40 3.7 and 2.6 times. The Enemies panel in the corner shows how strong they are now.
  • More bots every wave: 3 on the first one, 12 on the tenth, 25 on the twentieth. They come in a few at a time, and their aim gets better slowly: the first waves are there to get the hang of it. On wave 1 they deal half the damage and take more, back to normal by wave 5.
  • Their weapons change: only sub machine guns at first, then shotguns from wave 4, dual machine guns from wave 7, chain guns from wave 10, sniper rifles from wave 13, bazookas from wave 16, and flame throwers and photon rifles from wave 20.
  • A boss every ten waves: the eye, a big orb with more life that takes a fifth of the damage, with fewer bots around it up to wave 20 (from wave 30 they all come) and two escorts walking right beside it. It comes with the chain gun and the best aim of the wave: its bursts hit whoever stays in front of it, so run behind a wall and hit back when its gun runs hot and stops. Each time it comes back it is bigger, hits much harder and is a bit tougher.
  • Elites: from wave 10 some bots have a trait, shown by an icon before their name: a lightning bolt (fast: runs nearly as fast as you), crossed swords (brute: hits much harder and is a bit bigger), a crosshair (sharpshooter: hardly misses) or a shield (tank: takes much less damage). One bot in ten at first, more than half of them later on, and from wave 30 some have two traits. Every elite is a darker red than the other bots, a little bigger, with more life and a little more attack and defense.

Crates and power-ups

Wooden crates appear on the map: three new ones at each break and one more every 20 seconds during a wave, up to six at once. They stop bullets and rockets, so they are also cover. Shoot them, blow them up or hit them with the knives: each one drops a power-up that waits 30 seconds on the floor. Roll over it to take it.

  • Health: gives back half your life.
  • Grenades: 2 grenades and a molotov more.
  • New weapon: a stronger weapon falls on the floor. Press F over it to take it; it is yours until you go down.
  • Speed, rapid fire, shield and fury last 15 seconds: 35% faster, 60% more shots, half the damage taken and double damage.
  • Extra life: one more life, up to 5 (MAX shows on top).
  • Bomb: a blast around you that drops every ordinary enemy in sight within 10 blocks (elites and the boss lose a part of their life) and breaks the crates nearby.

Some power-ups are permanent: they last until the end of the run, and each one you take adds a level.

  • Fire rate, damage and speed: 10% more per level.
  • Resistance: 10% less damage taken per level.
  • Poison shot: whoever your bullet hits keeps losing life for 3 seconds, and the kill is yours.
  • Ice shot: whoever your bullet hits slows down for a second and a half. Poison and ice only come from bullets: fire, grenades, blasts and the knife don't carry them.

Cards

Each time you beat the boss (waves 10, 20, 30...), the game stops and shows 3 cards: pick one, and it lasts until the end of the run. At the end of waves 5, 15, 25... another choice comes, among the common cards only: the epic and legendary ones come from the boss alone. Taking a card you already have raises its level (up to 5), and one of the three cards is always one you have, to build your style. Sometimes it climbs 2 levels at once (the green card), and always when a new epic or legendary one shows up. The boss always brings a rare card (epic, or legendary from wave 40) and one of yours two levels up: the rare one, or two levels of yours? So every run turns out different. Some of them: Double Shot (a chance of a second shot right after), Parallel Shot and Fan Shot (more bullets), Piercing (bullets go through enemies), Critical (a 15% to 45% chance of double damage, not for grenades or molotovs), Vampire (life per kill), Berserker (more damage below 50% life), Dodge (an 8% to 32% chance that a shot misses you), Grenadier (grenades that come back by themselves), Kill Blast (an enemy you kill blows up and hurts the ones around it) and Ricochet (a hit jumps on to the nearest enemy). Rarer, with 3 levels: Shadow Allies (1 to 3 Orbs on your side with half of your upgrades, who fight near you, never get in your way or in front of your shots, and come back 8 seconds after falling) and Reflector Shield (every 10 seconds, a shield blocks all the enemies' damage and sends their shots back for 4 to 8 seconds, with a counter beside your grenades, and you keep shooting). From wave 40 the legendary ones may show up: Missile Rain (every 15 seconds, rockets fall from the sky on the enemies around you, without hurting you), Silver Bullet (now and then a bullet goes through everyone and drops ordinary enemies) and Necromancer (an enemy you kill may rise on your side for 10 seconds). Pick with a click or the keys 1, 2 and 3.

Cores and the Workshop

Every run ends with Cores, even the worst one. Each wave you survive gives Cores, more on the higher waves (17 for the first, 30 for the 10th, 44 for the 20th, 72 for the 40th), every 5 kills give 2, each boss beaten gives 60 and a new record 45 more. Leaving in the middle of a run counts too. Before you play, the Workshop (the Survival card on the start screen opens it) trades the Cores for permanent upgrades, with several levels each:

  • Health: the bar grows 20 points a level, from 100 up to 200.
  • Defence: 12% less damage from every hit, per level.
  • Damage: 25% more per level; fire rate: 20%; speed: 10%. Each level you buy takes you further than the run before. Grenades get half of the damage bonuses.
  • Starting lives: one more life per level, up to five.
  • Grenades: one more grenade each time you spawn.
  • Crate luck: 10% more chance per level that a crate drops a permanent power-up.
  • Crate healing: the life from the crates heals more (from 50% up to 100% of the bar).
  • Breather: some health back after each wave cleared (30%, 50% and 70%).
  • Second wind: a blow that would kill you leaves you with half your health, once a run for each level (up to 2).

At the top of the Workshop, your Power sums up how strong you start a run: attack (the starting weapon, damage and attack speed) and toughness (health, defence, shield, lives and the rest). A new player has 100, and each upgrade shows how much Power it adds before you buy it.

The Abilities tab has what only the Workshop gives. Each permanent power-up of a run stops at 5 levels; the Power-up limit allows 2 more levels per level bought, up to 21. And three abilities: the Dash (Shift, a burst of speed with a cooldown, during which nothing hits you), the Energy shield (takes part of a hit before your life does and comes back after 4 seconds without a hit) and the Shock wave (Q, hurts and throws back the enemies around you, its damage growing with the waves). On the high waves they make the difference. There is also Reroll cards: 1 to 3 rerolls a run, three other cards when none fits (R on the choice).

The Workshop also has a daily reward: come back every day for free Cores, more each day in a row (from 50 on the first to 200 on the seventh); miss a day and the week starts over.

The Cores and the upgrades stay in your browser and, if you sign in with Google, in your account, on any device.

Milestones, exclusive Orbs and checkpoints

Some waves are milestones: reach each one once and its prize is yours for good. On wave 10, the dual machine gun to start a run with; on 15, the exclusive Honeycomb Orb; on 20, the chain gun; on 25, the Flames Orb; on 30, the bazooka; on 40, the flame thrower; and on 50, the Crown Orb. The exclusive Orbs show up in the Orb picker of the start screen and work in every mode, online too.

Waves 10, 20, 30... also become checkpoints, opened once you get 10 waves past them: reaching wave 20 lets you start on wave 10, wave 30 opens wave 20, and so on. Starting further on gives you one permanent run power-up for every two waves skipped, but the skipped waves give no Cores.

Your record and the ranking

The record is the wave you were fighting when your last life went. On a tie, whoever reached it sooner wins: the time counts from the start of the run to the start of that wave, dead time included, and each death before it adds 30 seconds. Clearing a whole wave before its time runs out brings the next one sooner. A run begun at a checkpoint starts from your best time to reach it (kept for waves 10, 20, 30... and shown in the Workshop and in the Stats). At the end of a run you see the sum: the checkpoint you started from, the time played, the deaths' seconds and the total, with the checkpoints reached and a star where you beat your best time. It is kept in your browser and, if you sign in with Google, in your account; the Ranking on the start screen has a Survival tab with the best runs and the time of each. Esc pauses the run whenever you need.

Tips

  • Keep moving. The bots come straight at you, so a loop around the blocks of the map keeps them in a line behind you, where every shot finds someone.
  • In the Workshop, health and speed are good first buys: they keep you alive for more waves, and more waves mean more Cores.
  • Use the breaks to break crates and pick up what they drop before the next wave arrives.
  • Take the permanent power-ups early: a few levels of fire rate and resistance make the late waves possible.
  • Throw grenades where the bots come in, while they are still bunched up.
  • Keep the bomb and the fury for the boss waves or for when the map is full.
  • A crate between you and a sniper is a wall: let it take the shots while you deal with the closest bots.
